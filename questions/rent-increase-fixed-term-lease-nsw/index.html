Can my landlord increase the rent during a fixed-term lease in NSW?

Short answer

For a fixed term under 2 years, only if the agreement sets out the exact increase amount or method, such as a dollar amount or percentage. Terms like "in line with the market" don’t count. For terms of 2 years or more, rent can go up once every 12 months with 60 days’ notice.

Fixed-term leases at a glance

Your leaseCan rent go up during the term?
Under 2 years, started on or after 13 Dec 2024Only if the agreement sets out the amount or exact method, and no more than once every 12 months
Under 2 years, signed before 13 Dec 2024Only increases written into the agreement with an exact amount or method. These can apply until the term ends
2 years or moreNo more than once every 12 months, with 60 days’ written notice

Vague increase terms don’t count

Terms like "in line with the market" or "by the inflation rate" aren’t specific enough to allow an increase during a fixed term under 2 years.

You still need a proper notice

Even when the lease allows an increase, you must get at least 60 days’ written notice stating the new rent and the date it starts.

General information, not legal advice. For help with your situation, contact NSW Fair Trading (13 32 20) or your local Tenants’ Advice and Advocacy Service (tenants.org.au).
