Can rent be increased twice in 12 months in Queensland?

Short answer

No. In Queensland, rent can’t be increased unless at least 12 months have passed since the current rent became payable. The rule applies to the property, so an increase for a previous tenant or owner counts too.

The 12-month rule follows the property

If the rent went up for the person who rented the property before you, and that was less than 12 months ago, your rent can’t go up yet, even if you’ve only just moved in.

How to check the date of the last increase

  • Your tenancy agreement must include the date of the last rent increase.
  • You can ask your agent or owner in writing for proof of that date. They must give it to you within 14 days, and it’s an offence not to.
  • If the property is being rented for the first time, the "last increase" date is the date the agreement started.

If the rent goes up too soon

Increasing rent within 12 months is an offence under Queensland law, with a maximum penalty of 20 penalty units. You can ask the RTA for free dispute resolution or request an investigation.

There is one exception: if an owner rented the property below market rent (for example, to family), they can ask QCAT for permission to increase it sooner on the grounds of undue hardship.
