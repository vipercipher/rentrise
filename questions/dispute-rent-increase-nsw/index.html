How do I dispute a rent increase in NSW?

Short answer

If the notice breaks the rules, you don’t have to pay the increase. If the amount seems excessive, you can apply to NCAT within 30 days of receiving the notice.

If the notice doesn’t follow the rules

If the notice is too early, isn’t in writing, or doesn’t state the new rent and start date, keep paying your current rent and write to your agent explaining why. If you’ve already paid an increase that wasn’t allowed, you can apply to NCAT for the money back within 12 months.

If the increase seems excessive

NSW has no cap on the amount, but you can apply to the NSW Civil and Administrative Tribunal (NCAT) within 30 days of receiving the notice. You’ll need to show the increase is excessive.

What NCAT considers

  • rents for similar places in the same or a similar area
  • the landlord’s outgoings
  • the state of repair and amenities
  • previous increases, and improvements you’ve made

NCAT doesn’t consider your income or whether you can afford the increase. It can set the maximum rent for up to 12 months.
