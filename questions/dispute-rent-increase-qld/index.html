How do I dispute a rent increase in Queensland?

Short answer

Talk to your agent or owner first. If that doesn’t work, use the RTA’s free dispute resolution service, or apply to QCAT within 30 days of receiving the notice (and before a fixed-term lease ends).

Two different problems

If the increase breaks the rules, such as being less than 12 months after the last increase or without enough notice, it isn’t valid. Write to your agent explaining why, and you can ask the RTA for dispute resolution or an investigation.

If the timing is fine but the amount seems too high, you can apply to QCAT to decide whether it’s excessive. Queensland has no cap on the amount.

QCAT deadlines

Your situationApply to QCAT
Periodic leaseWithin 30 days after you receive the notice
Fixed-term leaseWithin 30 days after you receive the notice, and before the lease ends
New agreement at a higher rentOnly after signing it, within 30 days of signing

What QCAT considers

  • rents for similar properties nearby
  • the difference between the old and new rent
  • the state of repair of the property
  • the length of the tenancy and the time since the last increase

General information, not legal advice. For help with your situation, contact Residential Tenancies Authority (1300 366 311) or QSTARS tenant advice (1300 744 263).
