How do I challenge a rent increase in Victoria?

Short answer

Ask Consumer Affairs Victoria for a free rent assessment within 30 days of receiving the notice. If it finds the increase excessive and your rental provider won’t lower it, apply to Rental Dispute Resolution Victoria within 30 days of the assessment report.

Step by step

  1. Ask for a rent assessment from Consumer Affairs Victoria within 30 days of receiving the notice. It’s free. You can use the section on the notice itself or apply online.
  2. If the assessment finds the increase excessive and your rental provider won’t lower it, apply to Rental Dispute Resolution Victoria (RDRV) within 30 days of receiving the report. RDRV is free and tries to resolve it without a hearing.
  3. If it isn’t resolved, it can go to a VCAT hearing for a decision.

What counts as excessive

Since 31 March 2026, CPI is one of the factors considered, along with rents for similar properties, the property’s condition, facilities and services, improvements you’ve made, recent increases, and the rental provider’s compliance with their obligations. There’s no set formula.

Should you keep paying?

You don’t have to pay an invalid increase. However, Tenants Victoria recommends paying the increase while you challenge it, so you don’t fall into rent arrears if it’s later found to be valid. If the notice turns out to be invalid, you can claim back the extra rent.

General information, not legal advice. For help with your situation, contact Consumer Affairs Victoria (1300 55 81 81) or Tenants Victoria (03 9416 2577).
