Can my landlord increase the rent during a fixed-term lease in Queensland?

Short answer

Only if your lease says the rent will increase and states the new amount or how it will be worked out. You must also get at least 2 months’ written notice, and it must be at least 12 months since the last increase.

All four conditions must be met

  1. The agreement says the rent will be increased.
  2. The agreement states the new amount, or how it will be worked out.
  3. You get at least 2 months’ written notice (4 weeks for rooming accommodation).
  4. It has been at least 12 months since the last increase for the property.

Even when your lease includes an increase, it doesn’t happen automatically. The owner or agent must still give you a separate written notice.

At the end of a fixed term

You and the owner can agree to a higher rent by signing a new agreement, as long as it’s been 12 months since the last increase. If you don’t sign a new agreement, the lease becomes periodic on the same terms, and the rent can be increased with 2 months’ written notice given before or on the day the periodic agreement starts.
